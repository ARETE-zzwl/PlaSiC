# 4.3 Sea Interface { #sea }

This note describes the part of PlaSiC that connects the atmosphere to the sea surface: it collects atmospheric fluxes, averages them over the coupling window, and builds the surface boundary conditions that the next atmosphere step sees. The two updates it feeds are documented separately: the thermodynamic sea-ice kernel in [4.4 Thermodynamic Sea Ice](4.4-sea-ice-module.md) and the slab ocean in [4.5 Slab Ocean](4.5-slab-ocean.md).


## 1. State and sign conventions

For each horizontal grid point, the coupled surface stores:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $T_o$ | SST of the mixed-layer ocean |
| $T_i$ | Temperature seen by the atmosphere (ice/snow surface when ice is thick enough; otherwise $T_o$) |
| $h_i$ | Sea-ice thickness |
| $c_i^*$ | Continuous internal ice compactness used to expand or retreat the ice edge |
| $c_i$ | Coupling mask used by the current configuration (ice-free or ice-covered) |
| $S$ | Snow water equivalent on sea ice |
p
The main temperature limits are

$$
T_f=271.25\ \mathrm{K}\;(-1.90\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C})\quad\text{(saline-water freezing point)},
\qquad
T_m=273.16\ \mathrm{K}\;(0.01\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C})\quad\text{(snow/ice melting point)}.
$$

A positive atmospheric heat flux means that the sea-surface system gains energy. The ice kernels use the opposite sign for their internal ledger, where $Q_{ice}>0$ melts ice and $Q_{ice}<0$ forms ice; see [4.4 Thermodynamic Sea Ice](4.4-sea-ice-module.md).

## 2. Quantities accumulated from the atmosphere

`sea_prepare_step()` is called every atmosphere step. On ocean points (`land_mask < 0.5`), it accumulates:

- total heat flux, including sensible, latent, shortwave, and longwave terms;
- freshwater flux from large-scale precipitation, convective precipitation, and evaporation;
- solid precipitation, kept separately because it can become snow on ice;
- zonal and meridional surface stress and a friction-related wind diagnostic;
- the heat-flux derivatives used by the surface-flux scheme.

If the coupling interval contains $N$ atmosphere steps, each accumulated quantity is divided by $N$ at the coupling time:

$$
\overline{X}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}X^n.
$$

The default interval is one atmosphere step, so the average then equals the instantaneous value. Keeping the averaging step explicit is useful when a longer sea-ice or ocean interval is selected.

Freshwater and stress are passed through the interface for diagnostics and for the ocean module. The current slab ocean has no salinity or momentum state and no vertical or horizontal diffusion, so these fluxes do not create currents or change seawater density.

## 3. Returning the state to the atmosphere

After the ice and ocean updates, `sea_finish_coupling()` copies the new surface temperature, SST, ice cover, ice thickness, snow, snowmelt, and snow-change fields back to the atmospheric state and clears the accumulators.

`sea_update_surface()` then constructs the boundary conditions for the next atmosphere step:

1. it writes the actual surface temperature $T_i$ to the lowest atmospheric level;
2. it computes saturation specific humidity from $T_i$ and local surface pressure;
3. it blends the open-water and ice relative-humidity factors with $c_i$;
4. it blends open-water and ice albedo;
5. it computes open-ocean roughness from the Charnock relation and blends it with the fixed ice roughness.

For example, with $\epsilon=R_d/R_v$ and saturation vapour pressure $e_s(T_i)$,

$$
q_s=\frac{\epsilon e_s}{p_s-(1-\epsilon)e_s}.
$$

The grid-cell albedo is

$$
\alpha=(1-c_i)\alpha_o+c_i\alpha_i(T_i),
\qquad
\alpha_i(T_i)=\min\left[\alpha_{i,\max},\ 0.5+0.025(273-T_i)\right].
$$

The open-ocean roughness is diagnosed from surface stress and has a minimum value; the ice roughness is fixed. These fields affect the next step's momentum, sensible-heat, and latent-heat exchange.
