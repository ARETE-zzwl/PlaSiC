# 4.6 Three-Dimensional Ocean

The mixed-layer ocean (slab ocean) introduced in [4.5 Slab Ocean](4.5-slab-ocean.md) has only a single water layer with fixed heat capacity; it has no ocean currents, no stratification, and no deep-water heat storage capacity. It can reproduce seasonal-scale sea surface temperature memory, but it cannot represent several of the most important functions of the real ocean: transporting excess heat from the equator toward high latitudes, storing heat in the deep ocean through vertical stratification, and slowly releasing it on centennial scales. To supplement these processes while keeping the atmosphere model lightweight, PlaSiC attaches a **three-dimensional deep ocean** beneath the slab (`src/sea/3docean.c`; cf. Manabe, 1969; Bryan, 1969; Hansen et al., 1983; Fraedrich et al., 2005).

The goal of this note is not to retell a textbook, but to explain clearly, item by item, the equations, discretization, and coupling actually used by PlaSiC, so that readers can match them up when reading `3docean.c`, `physics_processes.c`, and `physics_io.c`.

!!! tip "Five things to remember first"

    1. **The surface is still a slab**: the SST seen by the atmosphere is provided by the slab, while the three-dimensional ocean handles the deep ocean below 50 m;
    2. **Temperature and salinity are prognostic variables**, horizontal velocity is a **diagnostic variable** (geostrophic balance + Ekman), and vertical velocity is derived from the continuity equation;
    3. The main processes of a single time step are: equation of state → diagnostic circulation → upwind advection and diffusion → convective adjustment;
    4. The slab and the deep ocean are **two-way coupled** through the top layer: the top layer is reset to the slab SST each step, and after one integration step the deep-ocean heat flux $Q_{\rm deep}$ is computed back;
    5. The three-dimensional ocean uses the **same Gaussian grid as the atmosphere**, is parallelized by latitude, skips entire land columns, and uses zero-flux differences at coastlines.

## 0. From Slab to Three-Dimensional Ocean: What Was Added

Recall the conclusion of [4.5 Slab Ocean](4.5-slab-ocean.md): the heat budget of the slab is only

$$
\rho_o c_{p,o} H \frac{dT_o}{dt} = Q_{\rm mix} + Q_{\rm deep},
$$

where $Q_{\rm deep}$ is a **prescribed** substitute heat transport, not something the model computes itself. The three-dimensional ocean turns $Q_{\rm deep}$ into a predictable interior ocean heat transport: it stores temperature and salinity in multiple layers, can form stratification, undergo convection, and redistribute heat vertically and horizontally.

When understanding the three-dimensional ocean, always distinguish two levels:

| Level | What it handles | Prognostic variables | Directly visible to the atmosphere |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slab mixed layer | Rapid response to atmospheric fluxes, provides SST | $T_s$ | Yes |
| Three-dimensional deep ocean | Stores and transports deep-ocean heat and salinity | $T(x,y,z)$, $S(x,y,z)$ | No, only indirectly through $Q_{\rm deep}$ |

Therefore, the three-dimensional ocean **does not replace the slab**; rather, it turns the slab from an "isolated heat capacity" into a "mixed layer covering a real three-dimensional ocean".

## 1. State Variables and Vertical Grid

### 1.1 Main State

Each ocean grid point stores one vertical column, containing `PLASIC_DEEP_NLEV` layers. Each rank stores only the columns of its own latitude band plus one ring of ghost rows.

| Variable | Dimensions | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `temperature` | $N_{\rm hor}\times N_z$ | Prognostic | Potential temperature (K) |
| `salinity` | $N_{\rm hor}\times N_z$ | Prognostic | Salinity (g/kg) |
| `u_velocity`, `v_velocity` | $N_{\rm hor}\times N_z$ | Diagnostic | Horizontal velocity (m/s) |
| `w_velocity` | $N_{\rm hor}\times N_z$ | Diagnostic | Vertical velocity (m/s) |
| `density` | $N_{\rm hor}\times N_z$ | Diagnostic | Computed from the equation of state |
| `layer_thickness` | $N_z$ | Constant | Thickness of each layer (m) |
| `layer_center_depth` | $N_z$ | Constant | Center depth of each layer (m) |
| `qflux` | $N_{\rm hor}$ | Output | Deep-ocean heat flux returned to the slab |

where $N_{\rm hor}=\text{NLON}\cdot(\text{NLPP}+2)$, and the two extra rows are the northern and southern ghosts.

### 1.2 How the Vertical Grid Is Generated

`deep_ocean_build_levels()` generates the grid by growing the layer thickness linearly. Using a 1-based layer index $k$,

$$
\Delta z_k=
\begin{cases}
50\ \mathrm{m}, & k=1,\\
50+40(k-2)\ \mathrm{m}, & k\ge 2,
\end{cases}
\qquad
z_k=\sum_{j<k}\Delta z_j+\frac{\Delta z_k}{2}.
$$

The first layer is exactly the slab's 50 m mixed layer, so that the "top layer" of the three-dimensional ocean is geometrically aligned with the mixed layer seen by the atmosphere. With the default 16 layers, the center depths are

$$
25,\ 75,\ 145,\ 255,\ 405,\ 595,\ 825,\ 1095,\ \dots,\ 4695\ \mathrm{m},
$$

and the deepest layer interface is about 5000 m, close to the global mean ocean depth.

![Vertical structure of the three-dimensional ocean and mixed-layer coupling](<../assets/images/ocean3d-vertical-structure.svg>)

Figure 4.6.1. The three-dimensional ocean lies beneath the slab. The left panel gives the layering and typical thicknesses; the right panel gives the slab heat budget, the top-layer reset, and the deep-ocean heat flux formula.

!!! tip "The number of layers can be adjusted by the user"

    The number of layers is determined by the compile-time macro `PLASIC_DEEP_NLEV`, and can be specified at build time with
    `make -C src run DEEP_OCEAN_LEVELS=22`; the build directory automatically gets an `_O22` suffix,
    avoiding mixing object files of different layer counts. The thickness formula holds for any number of layers.

### 1.3 Initial Stratification for Cold Start

At cold start there is no observed three-dimensional field, so the model fills it with an analytic profile (`deep_ocean_seed_state()`):

$$
T(z)=T_{\rm abyss}+(T_s-T_{\rm abyss})e^{-z/500},
\qquad T_{\rm abyss}=275.15\ \mathrm{K},
$$

$$
S(z)=34.7+0.2\left(1-e^{-z/1000}\right).
$$

That is, the surface takes the SST at that time and approaches 2 °C cold deep water exponentially with depth; salinity is about 34.7 in the upper layer and about 34.9 in the deep layer. Velocities start from rest. A restart run restores the real three-dimensional field from file.

## 2. Governing Equations

The three-dimensional ocean adopts an **incompressible, hydrostatic, Boussinesq-approximated** ocean framework (Bryan, 1969; Gill, 1982; Griffies, 2004). But PlaSiC makes one key simplification: **the momentum equations are not time-integrated**; the horizontal velocity is directly diagnosed from the density field. Thus there are only two prognostic equations: temperature and salinity.

### 2.1 Equation of State (Linear EOS)

Density is approximated with a linear equation of state (Gill, 1982; `seawater_density()`):

$$
\rho=\rho_0\left[1-\alpha\,(T-T_{\rm ref})+\beta\,(S-S_{\rm ref})\right],
\qquad
T_{\rm ref}=4\ ^\circ\mathrm{C},\quad S_{\rm ref}=35,
$$

| Parameter | Default value | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| $\rho_0$ | $1030\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ | Reference density |
| $\alpha$ | $2.0\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$ | Thermal expansion coefficient |
| $\beta$ | $7.5\times10^{-4}\ (\mathrm{g\,kg^{-1}})^{-1}$ | Haline contraction coefficient |

The linear EOS ignores the effect of pressure on density, so the "potential density" here is an approximate potential density. Its greatest advantages are simplicity and stability, and it is sufficient to drive convective adjustment and geostrophic flow.

### 2.2 Hydrostatic Balance and Pressure

The horizontal velocity requires a pressure gradient. Assuming hydrostatic balance $dp/dz=-\rho g$, integrating downward from the sea surface $p=0$:

$$
p_k=g\sum_{j\le k}\rho_j\Delta z_j-\tfrac12 g\rho_k\Delta z_k .
$$

The second term corrects the pressure from the layer interface to the layer center. Land grid points take $\rho=\rho_0$ and $p=0$, but they never enter the differences of ocean grid points (see Section 4.3).

### 2.3 Geostrophic Balance and the Ekman Surface Layer

On the rotating Earth, the large-scale ocean approximately satisfies geostrophic balance (Ekman, 1905; Gill, 1982). Writing the horizontal pressure gradient as

$$
u=-\frac{1}{\rho_0 f}\frac{\partial p}{\partial y},
\qquad
v=+\frac{1}{\rho_0 f}\frac{\partial p}{\partial x},
$$

where the Coriolis parameter is

$$
f=2\Omega\sin\varphi,\qquad \Omega=\frac{2\pi}{T_{\rm sidereal}}.
$$

$f$ is zero at the equator, which would cause division-by-zero amplification. The model limits $|f|$ to be at least $2\Omega\sin 2^\circ$, i.e. it considers the "effective $f$" to stop decreasing near the equator.

The sea surface also requires a wind-stress-driven Ekman flow. The model adds it directly to the top layer:

$$
u^{(0)}\mathrel{+}=\frac{\tau_y}{\rho_0 f h_0},
\qquad
v^{(0)}\mathrel{-}=\frac{\tau_x}{\rho_0 f h_0},
\qquad h_0=50\ \mathrm{m}.
$$

Here $u^{(0)},v^{(0)}$ are the top-layer velocities and $\tau_x,\tau_y$ the zonal and meridional wind-stress components. The form follows from the steady momentum balance in the surface boundary layer, where the Coriolis force is balanced by the vertical divergence of the turbulent stress:

$$
-f\,v=\frac{1}{\rho_0}\frac{\partial \tau_x}{\partial z},
\qquad
f\,u=\frac{1}{\rho_0}\frac{\partial \tau_y}{\partial z}.
$$

Integrating vertically from the base of the Ekman layer (where the stress vanishes) to the surface gives the depth-integrated Ekman transport per unit width,

$$
M_x=\int u\,\mathrm{d}z=\frac{\tau_y}{\rho_0 f},
\qquad
M_y=\int v\,\mathrm{d}z=-\frac{\tau_x}{\rho_0 f}.
$$

The model then converts this transport into a mean velocity spread over the fixed 50 m surface layer by dividing by $h_0$, i.e. $u^{(0)}=M_x/h_0$ and $v^{(0)}=M_y/h_0$, which yields exactly the expressions above. Because PlaSiC diagnoses rather than time-integrates momentum, this Ekman velocity is simply added to the geostrophic velocity already obtained for the top layer.

![The Ekman spiral in the surface ocean layer](<../assets/images/Ekman_layer.jpg>)

Figure 4.6.2. Schematic of Ekman transport. Ekman transport is the net motion of a fluid resulting from a balance between the Coriolis force and turbulent drag forces. In the picture above, the wind blowing northward in the Northern Hemisphere creates a surface stress, and the resulting Ekman spiral develops below it in the water column. The Ekman layer is the layer in a fluid where the flow results from a balance among the pressure gradient, the Coriolis force, and turbulent drag forces. Image source: [Ekman transport — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekman_transport).

All horizontal velocities are finally capped at $\pm0.5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ to ensure explicit advection stability.

!!! warning "Velocity is a diagnostic variable, not a prognostic variable"

    There is no $du/dt$, no $dv/dt$, and no Coriolis tendency or inertial oscillation in the code. Each time step re-diagnoses $u,v$ from the **current**
    density field. This makes the ocean circulation respond instantaneously (without inertia) to changes in stratification, and is the main difference from
    LSG (Maier-Reimer et al., 1993) or other primitive-equation ocean models.

### 2.4 Continuity Equation and Vertical Velocity

Under incompressibility, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf u=0$. Integrating the horizontal divergence downward from the sea surface $w_0=0$:

$$
w_k=w_{k-1}-\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\right)_{k-1}\Delta z_{k-1},
$$

then capping to $10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. The vertical velocity of the real ocean is also of order $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, so this upper bound is physically reasonable; its main role is to prevent diagnostic divergence errors from being amplified into spurious strong vertical transport after cumulative integration.

### 2.5 Conservation Equations for Temperature and Salinity

Temperature and salinity are predicted with **advection–diffusion** equations (Bryan, 1969; Pacanowski & Philander, 1981):

$$
\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}
=-\left(u\frac{\partial T}{\partial x}+v\frac{\partial T}{\partial y}+w\frac{\partial T}{\partial z}\right)
+\kappa_h\nabla_h^2 T
+\frac{1}{\Delta z_k}\left[\frac{\kappa_v(T_{k-1}-T_k)}{\delta_{k-\frac12}}
+\frac{\kappa_v(T_{k+1}-T_k)}{\delta_{k+\frac12}}\right],
$$

The salinity equation has exactly the same form. The interface distances are

$$
\delta_{k\pm\frac12}=\tfrac12(\Delta z_k+\Delta z_{k\pm1}).
$$

| Parameter | Default value | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| $\kappa_v$ | $2.0\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Vertical diffusion coefficient |
| $\kappa_h$ | $500\ \mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Horizontal diffusion coefficient |

Horizontal diffusion is used to suppress grid-point noise on the coarse grid; vertical diffusion slowly transfers surface heat and water-mass properties downward.

### 2.6 Convective Adjustment

Advection and diffusion may cause density to decrease with depth (hydrostatic instability). The model removes this instability using **convective adjustment** (Bryan, 1969; Marotzke, 1991): scanning from top to bottom, if

$$
\rho_k<\rho_{k-1},
$$

then the two adjacent layers are mixed, weighted by layer thickness:

$$
T_{k-1}=T_k=\frac{T_{k-1}\Delta z_{k-1}+T_k\Delta z_k}{\Delta z_{k-1}+\Delta z_k},
$$

Salinity is handled by the same formula, and then the density is recomputed. This scanning process is repeated until the potential density of the entire column no longer decreases with depth. It replaces the finite velocity of convective plumes in the real ocean with instantaneous mixing completed within one time step.

### 2.7 Freshwater, Freezing Point, and Clamping

The salinity of the top layer is affected by the freshwater flux $F_w$ (precipitation minus evaporation, in m/s). The freshwater introduced in one time step dilutes the salinity:

$$
S^{(0)}\leftarrow S^{(0)}\frac{h_0}{h_0+F_w\Delta t}.
$$

The temperature does not fall below the seawater freezing point $T_f=271.25\ \mathrm{K}$. To avoid numerical divergence, the code also applies conservative clamping to the following quantities:

| Variable | Range |
|---|---|
| $T$ | $[T_f,\ 320\ \mathrm{K}]$ |
| $S$ | $[0,\ 60]\ \mathrm{g/kg}$ |
| $\lvert u\rvert,\lvert v\rvert$ | $\le 0.5\ \mathrm{m/s}$ |
| $\lvert w\rvert$ | $\le 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m/s}$ |
| $Q_{\rm deep}$ | $\pm 2000\ \mathrm{W/m^2}$ |

These clamps are numerical safety nets; they are rarely triggered in a normal integration.

## 3. Two-Way Coupling with the Slab

This is the part of the whole design that most needs careful understanding, and it is the key to reading `physics_ocean3d_advance()` and `ocean_mksst_*()`.

### 3.1 The Slab Side: One Extra Term Q_deep

The heat budget of the slab is still given by the formula of [4.5 Slab Ocean](4.5-slab-ocean.md), except that $Q_{\rm deep}$ now comes from the three-dimensional ocean:

$$
\rho_0 c_{p,0} H\frac{dT_s}{dt}=Q_{\rm mix}+Q_{\rm deep}.
$$

### 3.2 The Deep-Ocean Side: The Top Layer Is Reset to the Slab SST

At the beginning of each step of the three-dimensional ocean, the top layer is set directly to the slab temperature:

$$
T^{(0)}\leftarrow T_s .
$$

This lets the 50 m mixed layer be governed by the atmosphere–slab system, and the deep ocean can affect it only through diffusion, convection, and circulation.

### 3.3 Computing Q_deep Back

After the top layer is reset, the model integrates the three-dimensional ocean one step, obtaining the new top-layer temperature $T^{(0)}_{\rm new}$. The difference between it and the slab temperature is converted into the deep-ocean heat flux:

$$
Q_{\rm deep}=\frac{\rho_0 c_{p,0}\left(T^{(0)}_{\rm new}-T_s\right)h_0}{\Delta t}.
$$

The meaning of the sign is:

- $T^{(0)}_{\rm new}>T_s$: the deep ocean has "warmed the top layer" after one step, indicating that the deep ocean transports heat to the slab, $Q_{\rm deep}>0$;
- $T^{(0)}_{\rm new}<T_s$: the deep ocean absorbs heat and the top layer cools, $Q_{\rm deep}<0$, and the slab is cooled.

This heat then enters the slab heat budget at the same coupling instant.

## 4. Numerical Methods

### 4.1 Time Discretization and Sub-cycling

Temperature and salinity use explicit time stepping:

$$
T^{n+1}=T^n+\Delta t\cdot\mathcal{T}(T^n,S^n,u,v,w),
$$

where $\mathcal{T}$ is the tendency from Section 2.5. Explicit upwind advection is constrained by the CFL condition (Courant et al., 1928):

$$
\Delta t\left(\frac{|u|}{\Delta x}+\frac{|v|}{\Delta y}+\frac{|w|}{\Delta z}\right)\lesssim1.
$$

The model subdivides $\Delta t$ into several sub-steps using a conservative estimate based on the velocity upper bound:

$$
n_{\rm sub}=\operatorname{clamp}\!\left(1+\left\lfloor 4\Delta t\left(\frac{0.5}{10^5}+\frac{10^{-6}}{50}\right)\right\rfloor,\ 1,\ 16\right).
$$

For a 30 min atmospheric step, this usually gives $n_{\rm sub}=2$. This is an engineering stability estimate, not the true CFL number computed step by step; it is conservative, so instability rarely occurs.

### 4.2 Spatial Discretization: Finite Differences and Upwinding

- Horizontally, the same Gaussian grid as the atmosphere is shared; the longitude direction is periodic, and the latitude direction uses extended ghost rows;
- Advection uses **first-order upwinding** (Haltiner & Williams, 1982): taking a backward difference along the flow direction, for example when $u>0$,
  $\partial T/\partial x\approx(T_i-T_{i-1})/\Delta x$;
- Diffusion uses second-order central differences;
- Vertical diffusion is written in the divergence form of interface fluxes, with the interface distances taken as the distances between adjacent layer centers.

Although first-order upwinding is only first-order accurate, it is positive-definite and does not generate new extrema, making it the most robust choice for a coarse-resolution ocean.

### 4.3 Land Mask and Coastal Differences

Horizontal differences must avoid bringing land values into the ocean. The model handles three cases (`zonal_gradient()`, `meridional_gradient()`):

| Neighbors on both sides | Difference scheme |
|---|---|
| Both ocean | Central difference |
| One side land | One-sided difference using the ocean side |
| Both land | Gradient set to 0 |

Advection takes zero flux at the coastline (not letting seawater flow into land); horizontal diffusion mirrors the land value at a land neighbor as the ocean grid point itself, which is equivalent to a zero-gradient condition. In this way, even if land grid points store arbitrary values, they can never affect the adjacent ocean.

![Horizontal decomposition, halo exchange, and land mask](<../assets/images/ocean3d-horizontal-halo.svg>)

Figure 4.6.4. The same Gaussian grid is blocked by latitude, adjacent ranks exchange ghost rows; coastal differences degrade according to the land mask.

### 4.4 Polar Treatment

The Gaussian grid does not include the exact poles. The northernmost and southernmost ghost rows have no neighbors, so the model uses linear extrapolation to give their sine latitudes:

$$
\sin\varphi_{0}=2\sin\varphi_1-\sin\varphi_2,
\qquad
\sin\varphi_{N+1}=2\sin\varphi_N-\sin\varphi_{N-1}.
$$

In this way the meridional difference $\sin\varphi_{k-1}-\sin\varphi_{k+1}$ remains finite in the polar regions and does not divide by zero.


## 5. Restart and Monthly Mean Output

### 5.1 Restart Records

The complete state of the three-dimensional ocean is written to restart, with records named

| Record | Content |
|---|---|
| `deep_nlev` | Number of layers, used for version checking |
| `deept` / `deeps` | Temperature / salinity |
| `deepu` / `deepv` / `deepw` | The three velocity components |

When reading an old restart, if these records are missing (for example, the single-layer restart of [4.5 Slab Ocean](4.5-slab-ocean.md)), the model **does not error out**; instead it rebuilds the three-dimensional ocean using the analytic profile of Section 1.3 and issues a warning. If `deep_nlev` is inconsistent with the currently compiled number of layers, it likewise falls back to cold-start rebuilding. The in-memory fields are halo-extended, and are packed into contiguous local slices before writing to disk (`physics_ocean3d_pack_local()`).

### 5.2 Monthly Mean NetCDF

The monthly output adds two three-dimensional variables with dimensions `(time, depth, lat, lon)`:

| Variable | Standard name | Units | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `thetao` | `sea_water_potential_temperature` | K | Three-dimensional potential temperature |
| `so` | `sea_water_salinity` | 1e-3 | Three-dimensional salinity |

The vertical coordinate `depth` is the real ocean depth (m, `positive="down"`), taken from the layer center depths generated in Section 1.2, rather than the atmospheric sigma coordinate. Land grid points are filled with `_FillValue` layer by layer according to the surface land–sea mask. It can be output with `--monthly-output DIR`, for example:

```bash
make -C src run --monthly-output ./out/monthly
```


## 6. One-Step Pseudocode

Compressing the contents of Sections 2–4 into pseudocode, for easy comparison with the source code:

```text
deep_ocean_step(config, ...):
    # 1. Sea-surface boundary
    for each interior ocean column:
        T[0] = slab_temperature
        S[0] = S[0] * h0 / (h0 + F_w * dt)
        clamp T[0], S[0]
    mirror ghost tracers

    # 2. Equation of state and hydrostatic pressure
    for all rows:
        rho[k] = EOS(T[k], S[k])
        p[k]   = hydrostatic integral

    # 3. Diagnostic circulation
    u,v = geostrophic(p) + Ekman(stress) at the top; cap speeds
    w   = cumulative horizontal divergence from the surface; cap

    # 4. Tracers: sub-cycled advection + diffusion
    for sub in 1..n_sub:
        tendency = upwind_advection(T,S,u,v,w)
                 + horizontal_and_vertical_diffusion(T,S)
        T += tend_t * dt_sub
        S += tend_s * dt_sub
        clamp; mirror ghost tracers

    # 5. Convective adjustment
    while column unstable: mix adjacent layers

    # 6. Compute deep-ocean heat flux back
    Q_deep = rho0 * cp * (T[0] - slab_temperature) * h0 / dt
```


The more complete level-by-level coupling flow is in [4.1 How the Spheres Are Coupled](4.1-how-the-spheres-are-coupled.md), and details of the sea interface, sea ice, and the mixed layer are in [4.3 Sea Interface](4.3-sea-interface.md), [4.4 Thermodynamic Sea Ice](4.4-sea-ice-module.md), and [4.5 Slab Ocean](4.5-slab-ocean.md).
