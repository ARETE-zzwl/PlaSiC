# 2.5 Grid-Point Nonlinear Dynamics

This note continues from [2.1 Governing Equations](<2.1-governing-equations.md>) and [2.4 σ Vertical Discretization](<2.4-sigma-vertical-discretization.md>) with the same governing equations and notation.

It describes what happens after the spectral state has been synthesized onto the grid: how the diagnostics \(A,\dot\sigma,q,T_v,\omega/p,\delta\Phi\) are evaluated at each Gaussian grid point and within a single column, and how the momentum, temperature, and moisture transports are constructed. The staggered grid, the discrete column integrals, and the interface flux differences it builds on are defined in [2.4 σ Vertical Discretization](<2.4-sigma-vertical-discretization.md>).


## 1. Grid-point adiabatic dynamics: turning the equations into analyzable fluxes { #gridpoint-adiabatic-dynamics }

After spherical harmonic synthesis, every Gaussian grid point already holds \(U,V,D,\zeta,T',\pi,\chi\) and
\(\nabla_h\pi\), where \(\pi=\ln(p_s/p_0)\). `calcgp` sits between "the spectral state has been synthesized onto the grid" and "the grid-point results are analyzed back to spectral space"; it performs only adiabatic nonlinear diagnostics at a single grid point or within a single column. It performs no spherical harmonic transform itself and does not complete the time integration.

Most of the output of `calcgp` is therefore only "grid-point intermediate quantities that make up the tendencies".

### 1.1 Moist geopotential correction

First recover:

$$
q_k=\max\left(\chi_ke^{-\pi},0\right),
\qquad
T_k=T_{0,k}+T'_k.
\tag{2.5.1}
$$

The virtual temperature and the moist-air specific heat at constant pressure (Emanuel, 1994) are

$$
T_{v,k}
=T_k\left[1+\left(\frac{R_v}{R_d}-1\right)q_k\right],
\qquad
c_p(q_k)=c_{pd}(1+\alpha_v q_k).
\tag{2.5.2}
$$

Here \(\alpha_v\) in the source is the constant `cpv_cpd_minus1`
\(=c_{pv}/c_{pd}-1\). Water vapor affects the adiabatic dynamics along two paths: \(T_v\) changes the density and the hydrostatic layer thickness, and \(c_p(q)\) changes the temperature response to adiabatic compression.

Here one must also distinguish the **full geopotential** from the moist correction output by `calcgp`. The linear hydrostatic part associated with the dry temperature and the reference state is handled in the spectral semi-implicit stage; the grid-point nonlinear stage only adds

$$
\delta T_{v,k}=T_{v,k}-T_k,
\qquad
\delta\boldsymbol\Phi=G\,\delta\mathbf T_v.
\tag{2.5.3}
$$

The construction of the matrix \(G\) is described in the [hydrostatic integration matrix](<2.6-semi-implicit-gravity-wave-solver.md#hydrostatic-integration-matrix>).
When \(q=0\), \(\delta T_v=0\), so this part of the geopotential correction is also exactly zero. The source parameter
`geopotential` stores the Robert-metric-scaled form of \(\delta\Phi\), not the full
\(\Phi\):

$$
\widetilde{\delta\Phi}
=2(1-\mu^2)\,\delta\Phi.
\tag{2.5.4}
$$

`gridpointa_products` then adds it to
\(U^2+V^2=2(1-\mu^2)K\); the spherical harmonic analysis removes the common metric factor, and the result finally enters the divergence tendency through
\(-\nabla_h^2(K+\Phi)\).

### 1.2 Using the same half-level velocity to construct the vertical transport

Once (2.4.6) has provided the half-level \(\dot\sigma\), the code forms four kinds of temporary quantity at every interior interface:

$$
\begin{aligned}
J^T_{k+1/2}
&=\dot\sigma_{k+1/2}(T'_{k+1}-T'_k),\\
J^U_{k+1/2}
&=\dot\sigma_{k+1/2}(U_{k+1}-U_k),\\
J^V_{k+1/2}
&=\dot\sigma_{k+1/2}(V_{k+1}-V_k),\\
H^\chi_{k+1/2}
&=\dot\sigma_{k+1/2}(\chi_k+\chi_{k+1}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.5.5}
$$



### 1.3 Constructing the horizontal fluxes of momentum, temperature, and moisture

The absolute-vorticity term, the vertical wind-shear transport, and the nonlinear surface-pressure gradient are combined into \(F_U,F_V\). The grid-point stage forms only these two components; it does not compute the final horizontal divergence inside `calcgp`. Those appear only after the return to spectral space:

$$
R_\zeta=\operatorname{curl}_h(F_U,F_V),
\tag{2.5.6}
$$

$$
R_D=\operatorname{div}_h(F_U,F_V)
-\nabla_h^2(K+\Phi+T_0\pi).
\tag{2.5.7}
$$

The horizontal transport of temperature and moisture is likewise first formed as grid-point products

$$
UT',\quad VT',\quad U\chi,\quad V\chi,
\tag{2.5.8}
$$

at every Gaussian grid point and at every full level. It is useful to group
these four arrays into two horizontal flux vectors:

$$
\mathbf F^h_{T',k}
  =\bigl(U_kT'_k,\;V_kT'_k\bigr),
\qquad
\mathbf F^h_{\chi,k}
  =\bigl(U_k\chi_k,\;V_k\chi_k\bigr).
\tag{2.5.9}
$$

Then the subsequent vector spherical-harmonic analysis differentiates the
two components on the sphere and returns the spectral coefficients of their
divergences, level by level:

$$
\widehat{\mathcal D}_{T',n,k}^{\,m}
 =\widehat{\nabla_h\!\cdot\mathbf F^h_{T',k}}{}_{n}^{m},
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal D}_{\chi,n,k}^{\,m}
 =\widehat{\nabla_h\!\cdot\mathbf F^h_{\chi,k}}{}_{n}^{m}.
\tag{2.5.10}
$$

The analyzed divergences are then combined with the local vertical/source
terms. For temperature, the explicit grid-point source
\(\mathcal S_{T,k}^{\rm exp}\) contains the \(D_kT'_k\) contribution together
with the explicitly treated vertical advection and compression terms, so

$$
\widehat{R_{T'}^{\rm exp}}{}_{n,k}^{\,m}
 =\widehat{\mathcal S_{T,k}^{\rm exp}}{}_{n,k}^{\,m}
  -\widehat{\mathcal D}_{T',n,k}^{\,m}.
\tag{2.5.11}
$$

For mass-weighted humidity, the source is the conservative vertical flux
divergence \(\mathcal S_{\chi,k}
=-\partial_\sigma(\dot\sigma\chi)_k\), and the corresponding tendency is

$$
\widehat{R_\chi}_{n,k}^{\,m}
 =\widehat{\mathcal S_{\chi}}{}_{n,k}^{\,m}
  -\widehat{\mathcal D}_{\chi,n,k}^{\,m}.
\tag{2.5.12}
$$

The use of \(T'\), rather than the full \(T=T_0+T'\), keeps the fixed reference
profile \(T_0\) in the separate linear/reference-profile terms described above.

In the implementation these two analyses are performed by <code>sht_mktend</code> and <code>sht_qtend</code>; their output is a
spectral tendency for each level, not yet the final time-stepped state.
