# 9.2 The Complete Mathematics Behind spat_to_SHsphtor { #spat_to_shsphtor }

`analyse_vector_level()` in `src/math_tools/sht_kernels.c` has one job: turn the
horizontal wind $(u,v)$ on the Gaussian grid into divergence and vorticity
spectra. Its central step is a call to `spat_to_SHsphtor()`.

The function name looks unfamiliar, but what it does can be summarised as a
simple chain:

$$
\boxed{
(u,v)
\longrightarrow
(S_n^m,T_n^m)
\longrightarrow
(D_n^m,\zeta_n^m)
}
$$

where:

- $u,v$ are the eastward and northward wind components on the grid;
- $S_n^m,T_n^m$ are the spherical harmonic coefficients of two scalar potentials;
- $D_n^m,\zeta_n^m$ are the divergence and vorticity spectra.

!!! tip "The key conclusion"

    `spat_to_SHsphtor()` **does not output divergence and vorticity directly**.
    It first splits the wind into:

    - a divergent part controlled by $S$;
    - a rotational part controlled by $T$.

    For each spherical harmonic mode, one then computes

    $$
    D_n^m=-n(n+1)S_n^m,
    \qquad
    \zeta_n^m=+n(n+1)T_n^m.
    $$

## 1. Why a vector wind cannot be transformed as two scalars

A scalar field such as temperature or pressure has a single value at each grid
point on the sphere and can be expanded directly in spherical harmonics. The
wind is different: it has both magnitude and direction.

If the coordinate system is rotated, the northward and eastward components of
the same wind vector change together. Transforming the two wind components
separately as scalar fields therefore does not give a representation of the
vector that is independent of the coordinate directions.

The remedy is to ask a more natural question first:

> Is this part of the wind moving along the gradient of some potential, or
> around the contours of some potential?

The first motion is described by a velocity potential, the second by a stream
function. Together, the two scalar potentials describe any sufficiently smooth
horizontal wind field on the sphere: this is the spherical Helmholtz
decomposition (see Swarztrauber, 1993; Dahlen & Tromp, 1998).

## 2. First fix the coordinate directions

SHTns (Schaeffer, 2013) uses mathematical spherical coordinates:

| Symbol | Meaning | Positive direction |
|---|---|---|
| $\theta$ | colatitude, measured from the North Pole | southward |
| $\lambda$ | longitude | eastward |
| $V_\theta$ | colatitudinal wind component | southward |
| $V_\lambda$ | longitudinal wind component | eastward |

Meteorology usually writes $u$ for the eastward wind and $v$ for the northward
wind, so before passing the wind to SHTns one must convert

$$
\boxed{
V_\theta=-v,
\qquad
V_\lambda=u.
}
$$

The minus sign comes only from the direction convention: $v$ is positive
northward, whereas $V_\theta$ is positive southward.

This is exactly what the code in `analyse_vector_level()` does:

```c
transform.grid_a[point] = -(double)transform.global_b[point];
transform.grid_b[point] =  (double)transform.global_a[point];
```

At first sight this looks like the components are swapped, but the letters `a`
and `b` mean different things on the two sides. On the PlaSiC side,
`transform.global_a` holds the zonal wind $u$ and `transform.global_b` the
meridional wind $v$, in the order in which they were gathered. On the SHTns
side, `grid_a` is the $\theta$ component and `grid_b` the $\lambda$ component.
Hence `global_b` ($v$) feeds `grid_a` ($V_\theta=-v$), and `global_a` ($u$)
feeds `grid_b` ($V_\lambda=u$).

## 3. The central idea: split the wind into two parts

On the unit sphere, SHTns uses the decomposition

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf V
=
\underbrace{\nabla_h S}_{\text{divergent part}}
-
\underbrace{\hat{\mathbf r}\times\nabla_h T}_{\text{rotational part}}
}
$$

Here:

- $\nabla_h$ is the horizontal gradient, differentiating only along the sphere;
- $\hat{\mathbf r}$ is the unit vector pointing from the centre of the sphere
  outward;
- $S$ is the **spheroidal potential**, which plays the role of a velocity
  potential;
- $T$ is the **toroidal potential**, closely related to the stream function of
  meteorology.

The division of labour between the two parts is clear:

| Part of the wind | Intuitive picture | Mathematical property |
|---|---|---|
| $\nabla_h S$ | motion down the steepest slope of $S$ | produces divergence, no vorticity |
| $-\hat{\mathbf r}\times\nabla_hT$ | motion along the contours of $T$ | produces vorticity, no divergence |

So the decomposition can be read simply as

$$
\boxed{
\text{horizontal wind}
=
\text{divergent wind}
+
\text{rotational wind}.
}
$$

The `sphtor` in the function name stands for **sph**eroidal + **tor**oidal. We
follow the terminology of the SHTns interface itself, spheroidal/toroidal, so
that it matches the output arrays `Slm,Tlm` one to one.

!!! note "Relation to meteorological notation"

    Meteorology often writes

    $$
    \mathbf V=\nabla_h\chi+\hat{\mathbf r}\times\nabla_h\psi.
    $$

    Comparing this with the SHTns formula gives

    $$
    \chi=S,
    \qquad
    \psi=-T.
    $$

    Hence the SHTns potential $T$ differs from the usual stream function $\psi$
    by a minus sign.

## 4. How the two potentials generate the two wind components

For any scalar function $f(\theta,\lambda)$, the horizontal gradient on the unit
sphere is

$$
\nabla_h f
=
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta}\,\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}
+
\frac{1}{\sin\theta}
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda}\,\hat{\boldsymbol\lambda}.
$$

Why does the longitudinal direction carry a factor $1/\sin\theta$? On the unit
sphere, a change $d\theta$ in colatitude corresponds to an arc length
$d\ell_\theta=d\theta$; a change $d\lambda$ in longitude lies on a latitude
circle of radius $\sin\theta$, so it sweeps an arc length

$$
d\ell_\lambda=\sin\theta\,d\lambda.
$$

The gradient is the rate of change per unit actual distance, so the derivative in the longitudinal direction is

$$
\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda}.
$$

Next, expand the gradients of $S$ and $T$ separately:

$$
\nabla_hS
=
\frac{\partial S}{\partial\theta}\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}
+
\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial S}{\partial\lambda}
\hat{\boldsymbol\lambda},
$$

$$
\nabla_hT
=
\frac{\partial T}{\partial\theta}\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}
+
\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial T}{\partial\lambda}
\hat{\boldsymbol\lambda}.
$$

The spherical unit vectors satisfy

$$
\hat{\mathbf r}\times\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}
=\hat{\boldsymbol\lambda},
\qquad
\hat{\mathbf r}\times\hat{\boldsymbol\lambda}
=-\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}.
$$

Therefore the rotational part of the SHTns decomposition is

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\hat{\mathbf r}\times\nabla_hT
&=-\frac{\partial T}{\partial\theta}
  (\hat{\mathbf r}\times\hat{\boldsymbol\theta})
  -\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial T}{\partial\lambda}
  (\hat{\mathbf r}\times\hat{\boldsymbol\lambda})\\[3pt]
&=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial T}{\partial\lambda}
  \hat{\boldsymbol\theta}
  -\frac{\partial T}{\partial\theta}\hat{\boldsymbol\lambda}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, add $\nabla_hS$ and $-\hat{\mathbf r}\times\nabla_hT$ and collect the
coefficients of $\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol\lambda}$:

$$
\mathbf V
=
\left(
\frac{\partial S}{\partial\theta}
+\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial T}{\partial\lambda}
\right)\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}
+
\left(
\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial S}{\partial\lambda}
-\frac{\partial T}{\partial\theta}
\right)\hat{\boldsymbol\lambda}.
$$

The two components can thus be read off directly:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
V_\theta
&=
\frac{\partial S}{\partial\theta}
+
\frac{1}{\sin\theta}
\frac{\partial T}{\partial\lambda},\\[4pt]
V_\lambda
&=
\frac{1}{\sin\theta}
\frac{\partial S}{\partial\lambda}
-
\frac{\partial T}{\partial\theta}.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

These two equations show that each wind component generally receives
contributions from both $S$ and $T$. One therefore cannot use $V_\theta$ alone
to find $S$ and $V_\lambda$ alone to find $T$; SHTns must analyse both
components together.

`SHsphtor_to_spat()` reconstructs the wind from $S,T$ using these two equations;
`spat_to_SHsphtor()` does the reverse, recovering $S,T$ from the wind.

## 5. What exactly does `spat_to_SHsphtor()` output?

As with any scalar field, both potentials can be expanded in spherical
harmonics:

$$
S(\theta,\lambda)
=
\sum_{n=0}^{n_{\max}}
\sum_{m=-n}^{n}
S_n^mY_n^m(\theta,\lambda),
$$

$$
T(\theta,\lambda)
=
\sum_{n=0}^{n_{\max}}
\sum_{m=-n}^{n}
T_n^mY_n^m(\theta,\lambda).
$$

Here $n$ is the total wavenumber and $m$ the zonal wavenumber. Substituting these
expansions back into the wind decomposition gives

$$
\mathbf V
=
\sum_{n,m}
\left[
S_n^m\nabla_hY_n^m
-
T_n^m\hat{\mathbf r}\times\nabla_hY_n^m
\right].
$$

This equation is the mathematical content of `SHsphtor`: the wind is not projected onto the scalar basis $Y_n^m$, but onto two sets of **vector spherical harmonic basis functions**:

$$
\nabla_hY_n^m,
\qquad
-\hat{\mathbf r}\times\nabla_hY_n^m.
$$

Hence the function call

```c
spat_to_SHsphtor(
    config,
    theta_component,
    longitude_component,
    spheroidal_coefficients,
    toroidal_coefficients);
```

performs

$$
\boxed{
(V_\theta,V_\lambda)
\longrightarrow
(S_n^m,T_n^m).
}
$$

It returns the spectral coefficients of the two potentials, not yet the
divergence and vorticity spectra.

!!! note "The $n=0$ mode"

    Since $Y_0^0$ is constant, $\nabla_hY_0^0=0$: the coefficients $S_0^0$ and
    $T_0^0$ do not contribute to the wind at all. They are therefore not
    determined by the wind and are set to zero by the transform. Correspondingly
    the $n=0$ divergence and vorticity also vanish, as they must: the global
    mean of a horizontal divergence or of a relative vorticity on a sphere is
    zero.

## 6. Why multiplication by n(n+1) gives divergence and vorticity

### 6.1 Divergence depends only on S

Taking the horizontal divergence of the wind,

$$
\begin{aligned}
D
&=\nabla_h\cdot\mathbf V\\
&=\nabla_h\cdot(\nabla_hS)
-\nabla_h\cdot(\hat{\mathbf r}\times\nabla_hT)\\
&=\Delta_hS.
\end{aligned}
$$


### 6.2 Vorticity depends only on T

Define the relative vorticity as the radial component of the curl of the wind:

$$
\zeta
=
\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot(\nabla_h\times\mathbf V).
$$

The curl of a gradient field vanishes, so only the rotational part contributes.
Using the standard surface identity
$\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot[\nabla_h\times(-\hat{\mathbf r}\times\nabla_hT)]
=-\Delta_hT$, one obtains

$$
\zeta=-\Delta_hT.
$$

The minus sign comes from the $-\hat{\mathbf r}\times\nabla_hT$ convention
adopted by SHTns in its decomposition.

### 6.3 Spherical harmonic space turns differentiation into multiplication

Spherical harmonics are eigenfunctions of the spherical Laplace operator:

$$
\boxed{
\Delta_hY_n^m=-n(n+1)Y_n^m.
}
$$

Therefore

$$
D=\Delta_hS
=
\sum_{n,m}-n(n+1)S_n^mY_n^m,
$$

$$
\zeta=-\Delta_hT
=
\sum_{n,m}+n(n+1)T_n^mY_n^m.
$$

So for every mode,

$$
\boxed{
D_n^m=-n(n+1)S_n^m,
\qquad
\zeta_n^m=+n(n+1)T_n^m.
}
$$

This is the origin of the two lines of multiplication in
`analyse_vector_level()`:

```c
divergence_value = -eigenvalue * transform.spectral_a[mode]
                   / transform.coefficient_scale;

vorticity_value  =  eigenvalue * transform.spectral_b[mode]
                   / transform.coefficient_scale;
```

where `eigenvalue = degree * (degree + 1)` is the Laplace eigenvalue
$n(n+1)$ of the mode (`degree` is read from `config->li[mode]`), `spectral_a`
holds $S_n^m$ and `spectral_b` holds $T_n^m$.

## 7. Four things most easily confused when reading the code

1. **The function output is not divergence and vorticity.**
   `spat_to_SHsphtor()` returns $S_n^m,T_n^m$; the divergence and vorticity
   $D_n^m,\zeta_n^m$ appear only after multiplication by $n(n+1)$.

2. **The northward component carries a minus sign.**
   The SHTns component $V_\theta$ is positive southward while the meteorological
   $v$ is positive northward, so $V_\theta=-v$.

3. **The SHTns $T$ is not $\psi$ in the usual notation.**
   In the convention used on this page $\psi=-T$, which is why the vorticity
   formula finally reads $\zeta_n^m=+n(n+1)T_n^m$.

4. **The Robert form and the normalisation factors do not change the
   mathematical chain.**
   PlaSiC uses the Robert form (Robert, 1966) and stores
   $U=u\cos\varphi$, $V=v\cos\varphi$, where $\varphi$ is the geographic
   latitude. Since $\sin\theta=\cos\varphi$, SHTns removes this factor during
   vector analysis and multiplies it back during vector synthesis. The interface
   must also convert between the two spherical harmonic normalisations through
   `coefficient_scale = sqrt(2*pi)`. 

## 8. Summary

The full name of `spat_to_SHsphtor` reads:

```text
spatial to spherical-harmonic spheroidal-toroidal transform
```

Its central task is

$$
\boxed{
(V_\theta,V_\lambda)
\xrightarrow{\text{vector spherical harmonic projection}}
(S_n^m,T_n^m).
}
$$

Then, using the Laplace eigenvalue of the spherical harmonics,

$$
\boxed{
(S_n^m,T_n^m)
\longrightarrow
\left(-n(n+1)S_n^m,\; +n(n+1)T_n^m\right)
=
(D_n^m,\zeta_n^m).
}
$$

So the complete mathematical logic by which PlaSiC computes divergence and
relative vorticity from the wind is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{horizontal wind}
\longrightarrow
\text{divergent and rotational potentials}
\longrightarrow
\text{divergence and vorticity}.
}
$$
